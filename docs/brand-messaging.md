# Brand Messaging Reference

**Source:** "Website messages" (drafted 2026-06-19).
**Status:** Approved messaging library for all website and marketing copy.

This is the source of truth for **what we say**. For **how we say it** — voice, color, CTA
styling, tone rules — defer to the "Brand and tone" section in [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md). Where
they overlap, this file lists the approved lines and CLAUDE.md governs the delivery.

> **Legal-claims caveat:** FormalizeIt is **not a law firm and does not provide legal advice**
> (CLAUDE.md key rule #6). Words like "lawyer-vetted", "legally binding", "pre-vetted", and
> "legally ready" set expectations — use them carefully, keep the "not legal advice" disclaimer
> prominent, and don't overstate. The standard NDA is founder-drafted pending counsel review.

## 1. Message hierarchy

Order every page and campaign by this hierarchy. Lead with speed; use reuse to explain *why*
it's fast; bring trust in only after the hook.

1. **#1 — Speed (primary).** The one thing every word exists to prove: you can send a legally
   ready NDA faster than you think. Hero tagline: **"NDA in minutes."**
2. **Supporting — Reuse.** Speed is possible *because* the legal text never changes: review one
   fair NDA once, then reuse it for every deal. This is the "why it's fast" story, not the lead.
3. **Secondary — Trust & collaboration.** Balanced/fair NDA, secure storage, audit trail,
   e-signing, teammates can contribute. Frame as "and it's safe", never "it's safe so use it".

## 3. Rationale / top-of-page explanations

Two approved framings for the "why this exists" line at the top of a page:

- **Option 1 (friction/waste):** NDAs are repetitive and low-value to negotiate, but they still
  create friction and waste time.
- **Option 2 (approve once):** Stop re-reading NDAs. Approve one fair NDA once, then reuse it
  safely for every deal.

---

## 4. High-level "how it works" (1-2-3)

Two approved variants of the three-step explanation.

**Variant A (fill-focused):**
1. Read the trusted template once.
2. From now on — fill in only deal-specific details.
3. Collaborate, sign, and share easily.

**Variant B (approve-and-reuse):**
1. Read and approve one balanced NDA standard once.
2. After that, generate, send, sign, and manage deal-specific NDAs without re-reviewing the
   legal boilerplate.

## 5. Feature framing

Approved copy blocks for feature sections. Keep the heading + one-line description together.

1. **One-time legal review** — Users or their lawyers approve the standard template once.
2. **Locked boilerplate** — The legal terms stay fixed, so users only fill in variable fields:
   parties, purpose, term, jurisdiction, effective date, etc.
3. **Fair by design** — Not "pro-discloser" or "pro-recipient," but balanced.
4. **Fast counterparty acceptance** — The other side sees it is standard, short, balanced, and
   not customized to sneak in weird terms.
5. **NDA dashboard** — Track sent, signed, expired, pending, counterparties, deal purpose, and
   renewal dates.
6. **Audit trail and e-signing** — Serious by default: every executed NDA is signed and recorded.

## 7. Positioning pillars

The durable ideas behind the copy. Every message should ladder up to at least one.

- **Speed** — NDA in minutes; done before the meeting ends; no back-and-forth.
- **Reuse** — one fixed legal text, reviewed once, reused for every deal.
- **Fairness / balance** — a single mutual NDA that favors neither side.
- **Trust infrastructure** — a recognized standard the counterparty already trusts, so they
  sign faster.
- **Security & seriousness** — secure storage, clear audit trail, real e-signatures.

---

## 8. Do / Don't

**Do**
- Open with the time win ("NDA in minutes", "Done before the meeting ends").
- Use reuse to *explain* the speed, not to replace it.
- Keep CTAs action-first and instant ("Send your NDA now", "Get started free").
- Emphasize zero legal knowledge and zero setup — accessible on any device.
- Cut any sentence that doesn't make the product feel faster or easier.

**Don't**
- Lead with team setup, configuration, or process language — that signals effort, not speed.
- Put trust/security ahead of the speed hook.
- Overstate legal claims — keep "not legal advice" prominent and handle "lawyer-vetted" /
  "legally binding" with care.
- Introduce jargon or delay.
